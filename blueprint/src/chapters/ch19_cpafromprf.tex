\chapter{Worked example: CPA security from a PRF}

Following Rosulek, \emph{The Joy of Cryptography}, §7 (CPA security). Encrypting
$m$ as $(r, F(k, r) \oplus m)$ for fresh randomness $r$ turns a pseudorandom
function $F$ into a chosen-plaintext-secure encryption scheme. When $F$ is a
perfect (bijection-family) PRF the scheme is perfectly IND-CPA: fresh randomness
each query reduces security to the one-time-pad argument at each drawn $r$.

\begin{definition}[PRF-based encryption]
  \label{def:cpafromprf}
  \uses{def:bijprf}
  \lean{CatCrypt.Examples.CPAFromPRF.BijCPAScheme, CatCrypt.Examples.CPAFromPRF.BijCPAScheme.toBijPRFFamily}
  \leanok
  From a bijection PRF family, the scheme encrypts $m$ as $(r, F(k, r) \oplus m)$
  with fresh uniform $r$; the underlying PRF is exposed for the reduction.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Correctness]
  \label{thm:cpafromprf-correct}
  \uses{def:cpafromprf}
  \lean{CatCrypt.Examples.CPAFromPRF.bijCPA_correct}
  \leanok
  Decryption recovers the message: $F(k, r) \oplus (F(k, r) \oplus m) = m$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Perfect IND-CPA]
  \label{thm:cpafromprf-perfect}
  \uses{def:cpafromprf, thm:bijprf-perfect, thm:otp-perfect}
  \lean{CatCrypt.Examples.CPAFromPRF.bijCPA_perfect_indcpa, CatCrypt.Examples.CPAFromPRF.boolXorCPA_perfect_indcpa}
  \leanok
  Built on a perfect PRF, the scheme has perfect IND-CPA security: advantage
  exactly $0$. Fresh randomness is drawn first and synchronized across the two
  games, and at each $r$ the key coupling makes the ciphertexts coincide.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Computational IND-CPA from a PRF]
  \label{thm:cpafromprf-bound}
  \uses{def:cpafromprf, thm:otp-perfect}
  \lean{CatCrypt.Examples.CPAFromPRF.cpa_from_prf_bound}
  \leanok
  Let $F$ satisfy the single-query PRF bound $\varepsilon$, and suppose the two
  PRF-swap hops together have advantage at most $\mathsf{Adv}^{\mathrm{PRF}}(R)$ for a
  distinguisher $R$. Then $\mathsf{Adv}^{\mathrm{IND\text{-}CPA}} \le \varepsilon$.
\end{theorem}

Replacing $F(k, \cdot)$ by a random function turns the scheme into a one-time pad,
which is perfectly secure (Theorem~\ref{thm:otp-perfect}), so the triangle inequality
for advantage composes the swap hop with the perfect hop. The swap hop enters as a
hypothesis here; Theorem~\ref{thm:cpafromprf-reduction} discharges it.

\begin{theorem}[Synthesized reduction]
  \label{thm:cpafromprf-reduction}
  \uses{def:cpafromprf, thm:otp-perfect}
  \lean{CatCrypt.Examples.CPAFromPRF.cpa_from_prf_reduction_gamehop}
  \leanok
  Let $F$ satisfy the single-query PRF bound $\varepsilon$. Then
  \[
    \mathsf{Adv}^{\mathrm{IND\text{-}CPA}} \;\le\; 2\varepsilon.
  \]
\end{theorem}

The distinguisher $R_A(y) = A(r, y \oplus m)$ turns the IND-CPA adversary into a PRF
distinguisher, and at each fixed randomness the swap advantage equals
$\mathsf{Adv}^{\mathrm{PRF}}(R_A)$. Averaging over the fresh randomness and bounding
each slice by $\varepsilon$ gives the bound; the factor two counts the two PRF
replacements, one per challenge world.

The single-query PRF bound quantifies over every distinguisher that sees one output,
with no bound on its running time. For one query that is a statistical statement: its
least $\varepsilon$ is the distance of $F(K, x)$ from uniform. It is weaker than the
standard multi-query PRF assumption.
