\chapter{Worked example: chosen-ciphertext security}

Following Rosulek, \emph{The Joy of Cryptography} (CCA security / authenticated
encryption). IND-CCA gives the adversary a decryption oracle on every ciphertext
but the challenge. The oracle is an ordinary function closing over the secret
key: decryption is stateless, so no oracle-state threading is needed. Unlike the
earlier examples, perfect CCA security is \emph{impossible} here (a decryption
oracle over the one-time pad leaks the key, and no perfect MAC exists over a
finite tag space), so the result is a reduction: for Encrypt-then-MAC,
confidentiality contributes nothing and the whole advantage is the MAC-integrity
gap --- authenticated encryption as confidentiality plus integrity.

\begin{definition}[IND-CCA game]
  \label{def:indcca}
  \uses{def:spcomp}
  \lean{CatCrypt.Crypto.SecurityDefs.INDCCA_Game, CatCrypt.Crypto.SecurityDefs.ccaDecOracle}
  \leanok
  The adversary receives the challenge ciphertext and a decryption oracle that
  answers every ciphertext except the challenge; the ideal variant answers with
  the always-reject oracle.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Generic CCA reduction]
  \label{thm:indcca-reduction}
  \uses{def:indcca}
  \lean{CatCrypt.Crypto.SecurityDefs.INDCCA_reduces_to_INDCPA}
  \leanok
  For any scheme, the IND-CCA advantage is bounded by its IND-CPA advantage
  (against the reject-oracle adversary) plus the two integrity gaps measuring how
  far the real decryption oracle is from always rejecting.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Encrypt-then-MAC is authenticated encryption]
  \label{thm:etm-cca}
  \uses{thm:indcca-reduction, thm:etm-perfect}
  \lean{CatCrypt.Examples.EtMCCA.boolEtM_indcca_reduces}
  \leanok
  For the one-time-pad Encrypt-then-MAC scheme the confidentiality term is exactly
  $0$ (its perfect IND-CPA security), so its entire IND-CCA advantage is the pair
  of MAC-integrity gaps: chosen-ciphertext security is precisely the
  unforgeability of the MAC.
\end{theorem}
