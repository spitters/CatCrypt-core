\chapter{Worked example: CBC mode}

Following Rosulek, \emph{The Joy of Cryptography}, §8 (block-cipher modes).
Cipher-block chaining encrypts a block $m$ as $(\mathit{IV}, F(k, \mathit{IV}
\oplus m))$ with a uniform IV. The IV is public, so masking alone does not give
perfect security; the rigorous argument is the same random-\emph{key} coupling as
CPA-from-PRF, which requires the block cipher to be bijective in both arguments (a
Latin square).

\begin{definition}[Single-block CBC]
  \label{def:cbc}
  \uses{def:cpafromprf}
  \lean{CatCrypt.Examples.CBCMode.CBCBlockCipher}
  \leanok
  A CBC block cipher carries two coherent views --- a permutation per key (for
  decryption) and a bijection $k \mapsto F(k, x)$ per input (for a uniform output)
  --- tied by a coherence law. Encryption is $(\mathit{IV}, F(k, \mathit{IV}
  \oplus m))$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Perfect IND-CPA]
  \label{thm:cbc-perfect}
  \uses{def:cbc, thm:cpafromprf-perfect}
  \lean{CatCrypt.Examples.CBCMode.cbc_perfect_indcpa, CatCrypt.Examples.CBCMode.boolCBC_perfect_indcpa}
  \leanok
  Single-block CBC from a two-sided bijection block cipher has perfect IND-CPA
  security: advantage exactly $0$, via the per-IV random-key coupling. (Multi-block
  chaining is only computationally secure --- a PRP reduction --- and is out of
  scope.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Computational bound from a PRP]
  \label{thm:cbc-bound}
  \uses{def:cbc, thm:cpafromprf-bound}
  \lean{CatCrypt.Examples.CBCMode.cbc_indcpa_bound}
  \leanok
  For a general block cipher with PRP-advantage $\varepsilon$, single-block CBC has
  $\mathsf{Adv}^{\mathrm{IND\text{-}CPA}} \le 2\varepsilon$ --- the real-or-random
  ladder swaps the permutation for random on both the real-true and real-false
  sides, with the uniform-IV masking perfect in between.
\end{theorem}
